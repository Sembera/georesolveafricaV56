A velocity contrast is the beginning of an interpretation.

In weathered and fractured terrain, the question is more useful than a single “depth to rock” number: how does the material change, where does stiffness increase, and where is more investigation needed?

The Rusizi investigation brings together measurements that respond differently to the same ground. Comparing them helps distinguish a plausible geological interpretation from one that still needs borehole or field confirmation.

Technical focus · SRT + MASW

Structure.
Stiffness.
Context.

SRT follows compressional first arrivals. MASW reads surface-wave dispersion. Together, they provide a better basis for asking what the ground can tell us.

01 / SRT

Where does velocity change?

First arrivals constrain P-wave velocity (Vp) along a section. SRT helps trace changes in cover and faster material at depth, guiding the interpretation of weathered zones and possible rockhead.

02 / MASW

How does stiffness change?

Surface-wave dispersion constrains shear-wave velocity (Vs). This adds a measure of small-strain stiffness, helping assess whether a faster P-wave response is also supported by a stiffer ground skeleton.

Compare

Two responses. One location.

Compare Vp and Vs over compatible locations and depths. Agreement strengthens an interpretation; disagreement directs the next question.

Question

Fast does not always mean stiff.

Saturation can raise Vp without a comparable increase in shear stiffness. Vp/Vs helps interpretation, but cannot uniquely identify water, clay, fractures or weathering.

Confirm

Evidence with boundaries.

Ray coverage, usable wavelengths and inversion choices limit resolution. Geological observations and boreholes remain essential for confirming material and rock quality.

Geological context · Karagwe-Ankole & the western rift

Old structures.
Variable weathering.
Changing ground.

The Karagwe-Ankole Belt includes metasedimentary rocks, metamorphic complexes and intrusions, with a long history of folding and deformation. Weathering and fractures add variability that a single velocity measurement cannot fully explain.

The younger East African Rift provides the regional tectonic setting. At Rusizi, the tentative geological drawings distinguish basalt, Precambrian basement quartzite and riverborne material. Regional context guides the investigation; site evidence determines the interpretation.

Technical context & further reading

Vs describes small-strain stiffness; with density, Gmax = density × Vs². It does not directly establish bearing capacity or rock-mass strength. First-arrival refraction may miss slower material beneath a faster layer; MASW resolution depends on wavelengths, aperture and mode selection.
