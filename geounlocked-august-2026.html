Project note 01 · Kabaya, Rwanda

Control starts
before take-off.

A two-day aerial magnetic deployment across two survey areas, built around a traceable instrument and flight record.

24–25 August 2026

The field record connects every magnetic reading to a time, a measured position and a known instrument.

At Kabaya, GeoResolve supported a two-day aerial magnetic operation using an RTK-capable UAV platform, a qualified pilot and the AeroSmartMag field system. Mission controls included operational permissions, payload preparation, launch-area management and sensor separation.

The archive retains raw magnetic files together with the flight-track record. Recorded fields include UTC time, latitude, longitude, altitude, projected position and navigation accuracy; the survey record identifies ASM sensor serial 103D1.

Those details preserve the evidence chain required to distinguish a ground response from avoidable aircraft noise, unstable sensor geometry or an inconsistent flight path.

The record preserves

  • Instrument identity
  • Acquisition timestamp
  • Measured position
  • Altitude and accuracy

Project note 02 · Kween, Eastern Uganda

A preliminary update separating what was measured, what passed quality review and what still required higher-resolution coverage.

By 2 September, the campaign had retained 230.8 production line-kilometres from a nominal 200 m NE–SW design. Each flight was matched against the approved route plan before interpretation.

Ferry legs and line-end turns were removed. Base-station behaviour, cross-line consistency and route coverage were reviewed, while available tie-line intersections returned 16.9 nT RMS repeatability.

The early grid separated quieter northern ground from stronger southern responses, with an observed anomaly range reaching 1,050 nT. The responsible recommendation was targeted resolution—not premature certainty.

Open routes should be completed and the strongest areas tightened to 100 m spacing through about 107 additional line-kilometres before target ranking.
